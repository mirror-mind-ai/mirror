# v0.38.0 — The Model Behind the Memories, Chosen by Measurement

**Date:** 2026-10-29

## Highlights

- The model behind memory and task extraction, titles, summaries, tags, reception,
  journal classification, week plans, descriptors, shadow, and consolidation is now
  `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`, at reasoning off. It was chosen on three measurements:
  the eval suite with reasoning on, the suite with reasoning off, and 24 of the owner's
  own conversations replayed through the product and ranked blind, where Haiku came
  first on 16 of 26 surfaces, ahead of Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite 17–9 and of GPT-6 Luna
  20–6.
- Every call on the extraction pin can carry a reasoning effort
  (`MEMORY_EXTRACTION_REASONING`: `none` to `max`), and a pin carries its own default:
  Haiku sends `none`, the setting it was measured with. A model named by
  `MEMORY_EXTRACTION_MODEL` sends what it was measured with too, so the rollback is
  exactly the request Flash Lite always got.
- Summaries open with their subject, carrying its status, instead of "The conversation
  centered on…"; and for the first time the title, summary, and tags prompts see the
  owner's name rather than `User`. A summary that still opens by describing the
  conversation is generated once more, with feedback.
- The release gate: `eval --all` 9 of 9 modules and 70 of 70 probes, the first fully
  clean run in the development home's history. Flash Lite had failed four probes in
  every recorded run.
- What you pay: about 40% more on these calls at typical volume, about a dollar a month
  where Flash Lite cost seventy-five cents. What you wait: about half a second more on
  reception, before a turn that then waits seconds for the answering model.
- No migration. Rollback is one line in `~/.config/mirror/env`.

## Where We Started

Since mid-2025 one model, Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, wrote everything Mirror writes about a
conversation after it ends. It dates from mid-2025, failed the same four eval probes in
every recorded run (duplicate memories across the two passes, a summary that reads as a
description of a conversation, a persona routed on an open existential question, a
trivial conversation given a title), and drew HTTP 429 from Google AI Studio on a third
of the eval suite's attempts. Two newer models cost about the same on OpenRouter. The
question was whether either wrote better memories for the person who reads them, and at
what cost in latency, reliability, and money.

The development guide treats a model pin as a versioned dependency and a swap as a
migration, measured before it is decided. CV9.DS9 was that migration, in three stories.

## What Changed

### Reasoning off, so the candidates could be measured at all (CV9.DS9.TS1)

Both candidates reason by default, and the transport sent nothing about it. Reasoning
spent the title call's 40-token budget, so 13 of 56 candidate title calls came back cut
off, mostly empty, and an empty title reads as a trivial conversation: silently
untitled. The blocking title-injection probe "passed" without an answer.

`MEMORY_EXTRACTION_REASONING` names an OpenRouter reasoning effort, sent as
`reasoning.effort` on every call that runs on the extraction pin, never on `consult`,
which picks its own model. A value that is not an effort is ignored rather than fatal,
because the setting is read inside the session-end hooks, where a failed call is an
extraction attempt and three quarantine a conversation; `runtime status` and
`runtime diagnose` show it. Eval records carry the effort, and `--history` labels a
flip across a model or reasoning change.

Re-measured at `none`, seven runs each: neither candidate had a call cut off in 1,428,
both passed every blocking probe 14 of 14, Luna kept every fix and improved two, and
Haiku lost one, the summary's opening, accepted as measured.

### The owner's own conversations, ranked blind (CV9.DS9.TS2)

24 ended conversations from the owner's production home, drawn by a recorded seed, were
replayed through the product's own functions: Flash Lite as it ships, Luna and Haiku at
`none`, one process per model. Kimi K3.1 Pro, a model from a fourth family, ranked every
surface blind against an anchored rubric, with the transcript fenced as data; the owner
ranked six conversations blind to calibrate it. The judge agreed with itself (0.83) and
not with the owner (0.55, under the 0.60 the plan required), so it did not stand, and the
decision rested on the owner's own rankings: Haiku first on 16 of 26 surfaces. The
judge's arithmetic, by a rule fixed before any output was seen, pointed the same way.
Nothing was written to either home, no transcript left the owner's machine, and the
evidence committed holds numbers and fixed labels only.

### The pin moves (CV9.DS9.US3)

The owner decided at the plan's approval: `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` at `none`.
Against it, weighed and accepted: about 40% more cost, because Haiku's tokenizer counts
1.5× the input tokens; reception about half a second slower at p95; and summaries
weaker as standalone notes than Luna's, though better than Flash Lite's, a defect that
turned out to be the prompt's.

One commit changed the pin, its reasoning default, and the price table together.
A pin's reasoning default is now a property of the model, looked up by the model the
call names: Haiku sends `none` whether defaulted or named explicitly; Flash Lite, and any
model not measured, sends no reasoning field. So the rollback reproduces exactly the
request Flash Lite was measured with. `runtime status` reads
`extraction=anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5, reasoning=none (model default)`.

The gate ran as the playbook orders: a fresh `eval --all` on Flash Lite before the
change (9 of 9 modules), then on Haiku after it (9 of 9, one expected probe failure),
every module's prompt hash equal, `--history` labelling the flip as a model and
reasoning change. The owner's first real conversation on the new pin came back titled,
tagged, summarised, and priced.

### The summary reads as a note (CR180)

The summary prompt told the model to write "a summary of the conversation" and to
"open with the topic the conversation addressed", then forbade writing "the
conversation" four rules later. The positive pull won: Haiku at `none` opened "The
conversation centered on…" in 10 of 14 eval runs with the prohibition in view, and every
Flash Lite summary in TS2 was flagged not standalone.

Three edits, each measured seven times: the first line now says who will read the
summary and why ("on its own, months from now, by someone deciding whether the full
transcript is worth opening"); the opening rule shows one example with its status in
it instead of describing what to open with; the standalone rule asks for what is now
known or decided, with the person called by name. Across 21 runs no summary named the
conversation.

The owner's validation conversation then failed anyway, and taught two more things.
First, the title, summary, and tags prompts had never been given the owner's name: the
close tail did not pass one, and the extraction path's resolver matched "You are
talking to" while the shipped identity template writes "You are speaking with", so
every lifecycle prompt since the port had labelled the speaker `User`. One fail-soft
resolver now serves both paths, and "the user" vanished from summaries with it. Second,
on a conversation that is itself about sessions and roadmaps, the model opens with the
frame about half the time whatever the wording says, and a rule that names the frame
gets echoed. So the guard is in the system: a summary whose first clause is the
conversation, discussion, session, exchange, chat, dialogue, transcript, or user is
generated once more with a short positive note, and the second is kept only if it
opens clean. A blind retry recovered 2 of 5; the note recovered 5 of 5. Both calls reach
the ledger. The owner's next conversation summarised as "<the owner's name> asked the
mirror to explain its operating modes… Settled: … Open: …", and the ledger showed the
guard had fired on it.

## Upgrading

```bash
mirror runtime update          # or: npm install -g mirror-mind@latest
```

No migration. The pin is configuration: the first conversation that ends after the
update is written by Claude Haiku 5.5. Your OpenRouter key needs no change; Anthropic
models are routed by OpenRouter like the rest.

Then restart your Pi sessions, or start a new session on Claude Code, Codex,
Antigravity, or Grok Build. The plugin copies Codex, Antigravity, and Grok Build keep
did not change in this release.

**To roll back**, put this line in `${XDG_CONFIG_HOME:-~/.config}/mirror/env` (in a
clone, its `.env`):

```text
MEMORY_EXTRACTION_MODEL=google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite
```

Then run `mirror runtime status`: the models line names Flash Lite with no reasoning
clause, which is the request it was always sent, and its calls stay priced. Rolling back
also brings back Flash Lite's upstream rate limits. Remove the line to return to the pin.
Nothing in the database changes either way.

## Conscious Exclusions

- One pin still serves every surface. The measurement favoured Luna for summaries and
  Haiku for everything else; a model per surface is a Change Request, not this release.
- The ledger does not record which reasoning effort a call carried
  ([CR181](../project/refinement/rs010-cv22-oracle-and-port-hygiene/cr181-the-ledger-cannot-say-which-reasoning-effort-a-call-carried.md));
  after the swap, the model column tells the two regimes apart in practice.
- No provider routing or fallback: the move retired Flash Lite's rate-limit exposure
  for the pin, and a rolled-back install takes it back.
- The extraction eval's prompt hash still excludes the summary prompt its probe sends
  ([CR184](../project/refinement/rs010-cv22-oracle-and-port-hygiene/cr184-the-extraction-eval-s-prompt-hash-excludes-the-summary-prompt-its-probe-sends.md));
  the `conversation_summary` module's hash covers it.
- A summary may still mention "the conversation" mid-text in about one of five
  meta-heavy conversations; the guard watches the opening, where the framing lived.
- Summaries already stored are not rewritten.
- Found on the way and captured, not fixed: a test that writes a synthetic consult row
  into the developer's real home on every `npm test` (CR182), and a Builder checkpoint
  card that once listed a record that did not exist (CR183).

## What We Learned

An LLM judge can agree with itself and not with the person. TS2's judge was
self-consistent and agreed with the owner's blind rankings on 55% of pairwise orderings,
under the bar the plan had set, so the decision was made on the owner's rankings
instead. Calibrate a judge with the person on four to six conversations before the full
run; learning it afterwards cost six times as much. The playbook says so now.

A prompt rule that names the thing it forbids gets echoed. "Do not describe the
conversation; record what it established" came back as "What the conversation
established is…". The fixes that held said what to write, not what not to, and showed
one example instead of describing. And when the content itself is about conversations,
no wording removes the attractor; a guard in the system does, in one retry, if the retry
says what the first sentence must do.

The biggest lever on real summaries was not the prompt. It was a name the prompts had
never been given, found because a validation on a real conversation failed after 21
clean runs on a fixture that had no name and no meta content. A fixture measures what
it contains.
