# The replay on the Navigator's own conversations — 2026-10-26 to 2026-10-28

CV9.DS9.TS2's measurement: Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite as it ships, and GPT-6 Luna and
Claude Haiku 5.5 at `MEMORY_EXTRACTION_REASONING=none`, on 24 conversations from the
production home. Kimi K3.1 Pro ranked their outputs blind, and the Navigator
calibrated it on six conversations. The full numbers are in
[`results.md`](results.md) and [`report.json`](report.json), both generated by
`score.mjs`.

**This directory holds no content.** No transcript, output, quote, or reason is here,
only numbers and fixed labels. The content check (`score.mjs --check-content`) passed
on every file before each commit. Transcripts, outputs, traces, verdicts, the review
page, and the calibration page are in the Navigator's `~/mirror-ts2-replay/` (mode
0700).

## What ran

- **The draw** (`sample.mjs`, seed `CV9.DS9.TS2/draw/2026-10-26`):
  - 24 ended conversations, from 329 eligible in the 60 days before the cutoff;
  - 16 with a journey, at most 5 per journey, and 8 without; plus one pilot;
  - nothing excluded, under the Navigator's consent;
  - the transcripts copied to scratch, and the database closed before any model call.
- **The replay** (`run_arm.sh`, `replay.mjs`), at commit `7f3d9265`, whose product
  code is TS1's `4a74f098`:
  - each model in its own process;
  - the product's own functions on every transcript: memories, tasks, title,
    summary, and tags with a journey; title, summary, and tags without one;
  - 104 surfaces per model in the sample;
  - every one of the 335 traced calls carried its arm's model and setting.
- **The judge** (`judge.mjs`): `moonshotai/kimi-k3.1-pro`, reasoning `high`, structured
  JSON, `data_collection: "deny"`, served by Moonshot AI.
  - Its prompt is the plan's appendix. The pilot tightened rule 6 (an invented-fact
    flag's evidence paraphrased instead of quoting), and the prompt was then frozen at
    SHA-256 `31f1de14…1de4`.
  - It judged 24 conversations and re-judged 6. All 30 verdicts were valid.
- **The calibration:** the Navigator ranked 26 surfaces of six conversations blind,
  before any result was computed. Two lines were corrected at the Driver's request:
  one ranking named A twice, and one conversation's five rankings repeated one letter
  order that stood for four different model orders.
- **Spend:** $3.90 in all; the judge was $3.25.

## What the judge found

Pairwise wins over all 24 conversations, summed across surfaces:

- **Luna over Flash Lite:** 75–29.
- **Haiku over Flash Lite:** 71–33.
- **Haiku over Luna:** 60–44.

Under the plan's rule, both candidates beat Flash Lite: each wins memories and two
of the other four surfaces, and carries fewer invented-fact flags (Luna 3, Haiku 16,
Flash Lite 26). Head to head, Haiku wins two surfaces and Luna none, so the rule's
arithmetic gives **Haiku**.

Per surface, in `results.md`:

- **Flash Lite** carries most of the flags: invented facts, trivial or duplicate
  memories, and every one of its 24 summaries flagged as not standalone.
- **Luna** is the most restrained, at 5.0 on restraint, and the least covering: its
  memories score 3.06 on coverage, against Flash Lite's 3.44 and Haiku's 3.38.
- Output length correlates slightly *negatively* with the judge's scores (−0.21 over
  all outputs), so the judge did not reward length.

## Why there is no recommendation from the judge

The plan fixed the condition: the verdict stands only if the judge agrees with itself
(0.83, met) **and** with the Navigator (0.55, not above 0.60). It does not stand, and
the plan's stop condition sends the result to the Navigator. **TS2 makes no
recommendation from the judge.**

**Decided by the Navigator on 2026-10-28:** accepted as measured. TS2 closes with this
record, and US3 decides the pin with the Navigator's own rankings as its main evidence.

## What the calibration shows

On the 26 surfaces the Navigator ranked, he and the judge often chose a different
best output for the same item. Agreement by surface:

- memories 7/12;
- tasks 6/12;
- title 9/18;
- summary 12/18;
- tags 9/18.

Their **preferences between models** pointed the same way:

| On the calibration's 26 surfaces | The Navigator | The judge |
|---|---|---|
| Ranked first | Haiku 16, Flash Lite 6, Luna 4 | Haiku 14, Luna 8, Flash Lite 4 |
| Haiku over Flash Lite | 17–9 | 19–7 |
| Luna over Flash Lite | 16–10 | 19–7 |
| Haiku over Luna | 20–6 | 16–10 |
| The longest output ranked first | 17 of 26 | 9 of 26 |

They differ in taste. The Navigator ranked the longest output first 17 times in 26,
and the judge 9: he values coverage more than the rubric's restraint does. Six
conversations are a small sample and the surfaces within a conversation are not
independent, so of these splits only the Navigator's 20–6 for Haiku over Luna is
clearly beyond chance.

## Operations and cost

Per surface in `results.md`.

- **Reliability:** Flash Lite drew 8 HTTP 429s in 112 calls, all recovered by retry;
  the candidates drew none.
- **Cut-offs and reasoning:** no call was cut off, and the candidates used 0
  reasoning tokens.
- **Latency:** the candidates are slower at the median on most surfaces; Luna is
  faster on tasks. For example, title p50 is 0.62 s for Flash Lite, against 1.19 s for
  Luna and 1.05 s for Haiku.
- **Input tokens:** for the same transcripts, Haiku counts about 1.4 times Flash
  Lite's input tokens, and Luna 0.9 times.
- **The production home's last 30 days of calls, priced:**
  - Flash Lite $0.77;
  - Luna $0.68;
  - Haiku $1.06.
